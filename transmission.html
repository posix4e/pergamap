The short answer

Yes—Arabic Galen has substantial international branches

The clearest routes currently in this map include Arabic Ars medica into both Latin and Hebrew; Arabic Galen on the pulse and the Hippocratic Aphorisms into Latin; an Arabic Alexandrian summary into Hebrew; and Arabic-only or Arabic-complete works made accessible in modern English and German. These are textual routes, not a general claim that every Latin or Hebrew Galen came through Arabic—many translations were made directly from Greek.

Evidence

Sources and limits

The local Greek and Arabic links come from Pergamap's versioned datasets. Later connections are deliberately selective and cite library catalogues, scholarly repositories, or specialist transmission databases. Dates often describe a translation or surviving witness, not the date of the lost exemplar.

Many Arabic versions were translated directly from Greek, while others passed through Syriac. Pergamap therefore marks the source-language route as uncertain for each local Arabic file until a cited, work-specific route is encoded. A missing Syriac node must never be read as evidence that Syriac was not involved.

Transmission data is a curated research aid, not a complete stemma codicum. Corrections with sources are welcome on GitHub.
