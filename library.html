Titles follow the conventional Latin of the Thesaurus Linguae Graecae and Kühn's edition; “Kühn” gives volume and, where verified, pages in Claudii Galeni Opera Omnia (1821–33).

Ḥunayn ibn Isḥāq and his circle rendered Galen into Syriac and Arabic at Baghdad in the ninth century, sometimes from Greek exemplars older than surviving direct witnesses. In the language of textual criticism these versions are indirect tradition — evidence about the text rather than copies of it — but a versional reading can descend from an independent branch of the stemma, and so belongs in it. Of any divergence, the question is which underlying Greek would account for it. Files come from the Digital Corpus for Graeco-Arabic Studies under CC BY-SA 4.0; their later branches are drawn in the transmission map.

Five of the translations have no Greek counterpart, the Greek being lost: De nominibus medicis, De partium homoeomerium differentia, De optimo medico cognoscendo, De partibus artis medicativae, De causis contentivis. For these the Arabic is not a check upon the text but the text itself. The first has never been put into English; a German version of 1931 is all there is.
